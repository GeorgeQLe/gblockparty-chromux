Research Scope Approved

working packet

The approved run covers Chromux plus the proposed GBlockParty managed-workspace/control-plane layer documented in this repository. It is intended to guide product priority and category messaging for technical founders and agent-heavy developers first, with a separate future-buyer lens for engineering and platform leaders.

The analysis tests:

  1. Whether the local cockpit and optional managed-workspace layer form one coherent category wedge.
  2. Which workflow advantages survive comparison across local, customer-hosted, and managed environments.
  3. Ecosystem fit across AI development surfaces, remote workspaces, autonomous agents, and browser/runtime infrastructure.
  4. Supportable current trust claims versus proposed future trust requirements.
  5. Adoption and switching costs for individual operators and later platform buyers.

Non-goals are pricing recommendations, implementation scheduling, unsupported security/scale/enterprise claims, and additional GBlock Party products without authoritative sources.

Approved output paths:

  • Active working packet: research/_working/preliminary-devtool-positioning-research.md
  • Supporting evidence log: research/_working/devtool-positioning-evidence-log.md
  • Stage 2 review page: alignment/devtool-positioning-gblockparty.html

Executive Findings

working packet

1. The combined system is coherent only when the managed layer extends Chromux’s exact supervision model

Claim: Chromux and the proposed GBlockParty layer can form one product system, but “local cockpit plus cloud workspaces” is too generic. The coherent thread is continuity of the same supervised agent-work model: session identity, terminal, browser surface, attention state, evidence artifacts, approvals, and interventions remain bound together across hosts and devices.

Evidence: Chromux already implements session-scoped attention, preview approval, browser state, capture evidence, restore metadata, and resource/capture approval. The proposed architecture carries Session, Surface, Artifact, Lease, and Event resources across local, customer, and managed hosts. Meanwhile Cursor, Claude, Coder, and others already claim local-cloud or hybrid continuity.

Inference: Infrastructure continuity is not the differentiator; continuity of Chromux’s operator semantics is.

Confidence: High for the strategic distinction; medium that users will value the combined system; low that the proposed managed layer can deliver it because it is not implemented.

Decision impact: Position the current product around agent supervision. Present managed workspaces as an optional extension, not the lead category or proof of current capability.

2. “Workspace,” “browser,” “parallel agents,” and “local-to-cloud” are table stakes or occupied categories

Claim: None of those nouns can carry the positioning alone.

Evidence: Cursor documents parallel agents, an Agents Window across local/cloud/remote environments, browser tooling, and local-cloud handoff. Claude spans terminal, IDE, desktop, web, mobile/remote-control, and Chrome. Coder positions as provider-neutral infrastructure for agents and cloud development environments, including external workspaces. Devin combines shell, IDE, browser, proof artifacts, and human takeover. Browserbase, Playwright, and Chrome DevTools cover browser infrastructure and observability.

Inference: A broad “agent workspace” message places GBlockParty against larger products on their strongest ground and hides the shipped behavior that is more specific.

Confidence: High.

Decision impact: Use these as supporting capabilities. Do not make them the headline differentiator.

3. The defensible wedge is provider-neutral, session-bound supervision and intervention

Claim: Chromux’s strongest current advantage is the exact binding of an unchanged terminal-native agent to its own terminal, browser context, attention state, and explicit evidence/intervention path.

Evidence: The app supports Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, and shell processes without replacing their runtimes. Preview URLs enter approval queues instead of stealing focus. Attention is projected per session. Captures are inspectable, bounded, and routed with originating session metadata. Full-browser Composer can steer an existing agent or stage a new session while keeping evidence explicit.

Inference: Competitors often optimize a single vendor, an IDE, autonomous task completion, generic workspace lifecycle, or browser automation. Chromux can own the operator’s problem: “Which agent needs me, what was it looking at, and how do I send the right intervention back without losing context?”

Confidence: High that the behavior exists; medium that it is durable differentiation; no external validation of willingness to switch.

Decision impact: Lead with “agent operations cockpit” or “local-first control room,” then prove it with the session-terminal-browser-attention-evidence binding.

4. Trust is a product behavior, not an adjective

Claim: The shipped trust story is strongest when stated as concrete controls: account-optional local mode, no Chromux capture upload or product telemetry, approval-gated previews, one-time capture consent, inspectable local artifacts, private artifact permissions, and unchanged provider credentials.

Evidence: Repository privacy and capture documentation records those behaviors and their limits. It also records GitHub update checks, local plaintext history, persistent browser/profile data, and captures that are never automatically deleted.

Inference: “Private,” “secure,” or “enterprise-ready” would erase meaningful caveats. Behavioral language is more credible and differentiating.

Confidence: High for current documented behavior.

Decision impact: Make claims auditable and bounded. Keep managed encryption, device trust, RBAC, audit, policy, retention, and recovery explicitly proposed until implemented and tested.

5. The two audiences require different messages and proof

Claim: Individual operators buy less context switching and safer intervention; platform leaders buy policy, lifecycle, deployment, identity, audit, and cost control.

Evidence: Chromux’s shipped workflow addresses the first. Coder, Codespaces, and enterprise agent products set the second audience’s expectations. GBlockParty’s control-plane document lists those future requirements but provides no implementation or commercial proof.

Inference: Collapsing both audiences produces vague “workspace platform” language and overstates maturity.

Confidence: High.

Decision impact: Keep the primary message individual and workflow-led. Treat the platform-buyer message as a future architecture thesis with a separate evidence burden.

Recommended Positioning

working packet

Category

Primary category: local-first agent operations cockpit.

Supporting category: optional managed continuity for agent workspaces.

Avoid leading with:

  • AI IDE
  • cloud development environment
  • autonomous software engineer
  • browser automation platform
  • generic agent workspace
  • enterprise agent infrastructure

Positioning Statement

GBlockParty is the local-first operating layer for supervised agent development. Chromux keeps each coding agent tied to its terminal, browser, attention state, and evidence; optional managed workspaces extend that same operating model across devices and hosts.

Product-Level Statement

GBlockParty Chromux is a local-first control room for developers running multiple coding agents. It keeps each agent tied to the right terminal, browser, and evidence so you can see what needs attention and intervene without losing context.

Managed-Layer Statement

When managed workspaces arrive, GBlockParty will extend Chromux’s session, surface, evidence, and approval model across registered local machines, customer-managed hosts, and GBlockParty runners.

This must be labeled as direction, not current availability.

Short Tagline Options

  1. Keep every agent, browser, and intervention in the right context. — Recommended; concrete and workflow-led.
  2. The local-first control room for coding agents. — Clear category language; less explicit about the evidence loop.
  3. Supervise agent work across terminals, browsers, and hosts. — Best after managed continuity exists.

One-Sentence Contrast

Unlike single-vendor agent surfaces, generic cloud workspaces, or browser-automation infrastructure, Chromux preserves native CLI choice and organizes the human supervision loop around each agent’s live terminal, browser, attention, and evidence.

This is a workflow contrast, not a claim that competitors lack browsers, terminals, parallelism, handoff, or human review.

Workflow Advantage

working packet

The core job

The user’s recurring job is not merely “run agents” or “open a preview.” It is:

  1. Start or adopt several native agent sessions without changing provider habits.
  2. Know which session is working, blocked, complete, or holding a preview or failed delivery.
  3. Open the exact browser context associated with that session without focus theft.
  4. Inspect or capture bounded evidence.
  5. Send the intervention to the correct existing session or deliberately create a new one.
  6. Recover the operator’s state after restart.
  7. Eventually, perform the same supervision from another device without transferring provider credentials into a generic sync layer.

Unique combination

No single element is unique. The combination is the proposed advantage:

  • multi-provider native CLI preservation;
  • one-to-one session and browser ownership;
  • unified but session-scoped attention;
  • approval-gated preview routing;
  • explicit, inspectable evidence artifacts;
  • intervention routed to a named session;
  • local account-optional operation;
  • future host/device continuity that retains the same resource and approval semantics.

What the product should prove

The positioning becomes credible when demos and product evidence show:

  1. Three different agent providers running side by side.
  2. A background session requesting input while another completes and a third queues a preview.
  3. The operator jumping directly to the right session and browser.
  4. A capture or attachment reaching the correct session with visible provenance.
  5. No automatic page opening, capture, upload, or hidden credential transfer.
  6. Later, a second device attaching to the same live workspace with single-writer input control.

Ecosystem Fit

working packet

Agent providers

Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, and shell remain native host processes. GBlockParty should not become a model router or credential owner to justify its category. Provider adapters should remain explicit and replaceable.

Browser ecosystem

Playwright, Chrome DevTools, and Browserbase are complements or future adapters. Chromux should own operator presentation, approval, provenance, and routing; it should reuse established automation/debugging primitives where they improve reliability.

Workspace ecosystem

Dev Containers, Coder templates, Codespaces, customer VMs, and managed runners can become host implementations or integration targets. GBlockParty should own stable workspace/session/surface/artifact/lease/event semantics, not insist on one provisioning substrate.

Protocols

MCP is useful for exposing approved resources and capture tools, and Chromux already has a local resource-broker MCP surface. It is an integration channel, not the product positioning.

Git and worktrees

Repository identity, revision/worktree identity, and exact provider session IDs are essential for portable continuity. Absolute working directory alone is not a durable workspace identity.

Trust Claims

working packet

Supportable now

  • Local Chromux works without a GBlockParty account.
  • Chromux does not provide hosted sync, capture upload, or product telemetry.
  • Agent CLIs keep their own credentials and runtimes.
  • Preview discovery is approval-gated; detected pages do not auto-open.
  • Capture is explicit; MCP screenshot/recording requests require visible one-time approval.
  • Evidence payloads are bounded, inspectable local files.
  • Capture resources are limited by artifact manifests and private local permissions.
  • Update checks contact GitHub Releases.

Required caveats

  • Browser profiles, prompt history, restore state, captures, screenshots, and recordings can contain sensitive data.
  • Captures are not automatically deleted.
  • Some history is local plaintext.
  • An approved MCP client receives image bytes or local resource access.
  • Local-first does not mean “nothing ever leaves the machine”; agent providers and GitHub update checks have their own network paths.
  • Windows agent sessions depend on WSL2 and the user’s installed/authenticated CLIs.

Proposed only

  • Tenant-scoped encryption.
  • Device trust and revocable credentials.
  • Organization authorization, RBAC, policy, audit, budgets, and quotas.
  • Short-lived input leases across devices.
  • Managed artifact retention, deletion, export, and recovery.
  • Persistent hosted processes, browsers, preview tunnels, and runners.

Claims to avoid

  • “Secure by default” without a threat model and independent testing.
  • “Enterprise-ready.”
  • “Seamless migration” or “lossless continuation.”
  • “Runs anywhere” before host compatibility is demonstrated.
  • “Works with any agent.”
  • “Private cloud” without deployed architecture and operational controls.
  • “Unique browser automation,” “unique parallel agents,” or “the first agent workspace.”

Switching Cost

working packet

Technical founder / agent-heavy developer

Costs:

  • install and Electron/Chromium footprint;
  • another navigation model and shortcut set;
  • screen-space competition;
  • CLI discovery/auth and Windows WSL setup;
  • trust in local storage and browser profiles;
  • risk of wrong-session routing;
  • giving up integrated IDE or single-vendor cloud-agent conveniences.

Offsets:

  • unchanged provider CLIs and credentials;
  • shell fallback and explicit controls;
  • local account-optional mode;
  • read-only adoption/resume paths;
  • inspectable payloads and clear session provenance;
  • one cockpit across several providers.

Adoption threshold: one repeated workflow must be materially faster and safer than terminal-plus-browser—most likely triaging several concurrent sessions and sending browser evidence to the right one.

Engineering / platform leader

Costs:

  • host daemon and network integration;
  • workspace templates/images and repository access;
  • identity, secret, policy, audit, retention, and incident-response review;
  • data residency and browser-session risk;
  • overlap with Coder, Codespaces, internal platforms, or existing agent vendors;
  • uncertain vendor maturity and support.

Offsets, once proven:

  • provider-neutral operator plane;
  • one resource/event model across local, customer, and managed hosts;
  • explicit input leases and intervention semantics;
  • inspectable evidence and attention history;
  • incremental adoption that preserves local workflows.

Adoption threshold: independently demonstrated security/operations, a stable API/protocol, and evidence that the operator model improves multi-agent throughput or control. This threshold is not met today.

Audience-Specific Messaging

working packet

Primary user

Problem: “I have several agents and previews open; I need to know which one needs me and intervene in the right context.”

Promise: One local control room that keeps each native agent tied to its terminal, browser, attention, and evidence.

Proof: Multi-provider sessions, attention queue, approval-gated previews, explicit capture, routed Composer, restore.

CTA direction: Run the same CLI agents you already use; supervise them in Chromux.

Future platform buyer

Problem: “We need agent workspaces to stay reachable, governed, and recoverable across infrastructure without forcing one agent vendor.”

Promise: Extend Chromux’s operator model across registered and managed hosts.

Proof required before claim: deployed host protocol, identity and authorization, leases, encrypted storage, audit, retention, recovery tests, scale/availability data, and customer evidence.

Current message status: architecture thesis, not sales positioning.

Alternatives, Rejected Findings, And Lower-Confidence Findings

working packet

Rejected

  • “The category is local-first agent workspace.” Too broad; Coder, Cursor, Codespaces, OpenHands, and vendor ecosystems occupy workspace/runtime language.
  • “Local-to-cloud continuity is the wedge.” Cursor and Claude already document handoff or remote continuation, while Coder covers hybrid infrastructure.
  • “Browser evidence is unique.” Cursor, Devin, Browserbase, Playwright, and Chrome DevTools provide strong browser proof and observability.
  • “Parallel agents are unique.” This is an active competitor surface.
  • “Managed infrastructure makes the current product enterprise-ready.” The layer is proposed only.
  • “GBlockParty should compete as an autonomous agent.” It should orchestrate existing agents and preserve provider choice.

Lower confidence

  • “Agent operations cockpit” will become recognized category language. It is strategically descriptive but not validated buyer language.
  • The local and managed products will feel like one system. This depends on shipping shared identity, event, session, surface, artifact, and lease semantics.
  • Provider neutrality creates enough value to pull users away from Cursor/Claude/Codex ecosystems. There is no external switching evidence.
  • Platform leaders will value the operator model over established CDE platforms. No buyer evidence exists.
  • Grok Build support is a durable acquisition differentiator. It broadens neutrality but has specific data-risk caveats and unknown demand.

Downstream Implications

working packet

Product

  • Make session/terminal/browser/attention/evidence binding the product invariant.
  • Keep native provider CLIs and exact session identity visible.
  • Treat the managed host protocol as an extension of existing resource semantics.
  • Avoid generic workspace features that do not strengthen supervision.

Brand and messaging

  • Lead with the operator’s supervision job.
  • Demonstrate several providers and concurrent attention states.
  • Use “optional managed workspaces” as supporting future direction.
  • Separate shipped proof from roadmap language visually and verbally.

Research

  • Interview multi-agent CLI users about coordination, wrong-session errors, preview/evidence routing, and provider switching.
  • Run message tests for “agent operations cockpit,” “control room,” and concrete job language.
  • Compare one real workflow hands-on against manual terminal+browser, Cursor, and one vendor-native surface.
  • Interview platform teams only after a host-protocol proof exists.

Trust and documentation

  • Keep the privacy inventory current as storage and capture surfaces evolve.
  • Add user-facing retention controls before making stronger privacy claims.
  • Publish a threat model before managed or enterprise positioning.
  • Treat browser content and evidence as untrusted/sensitive across local and managed modes.
